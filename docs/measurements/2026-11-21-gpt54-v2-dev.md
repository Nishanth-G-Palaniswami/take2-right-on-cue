## D3: the counting rule, judge verdict and human label disagree

Both replies ask what the applicant did and how it was resolved, as one request. The counting rule counts that as asking for the outcome, and Nishanth accepted both. judge-v1 scored next action 1 on both:

- **D3-B.** The reply "asks both what Riley said/did and how it ended up being resolved, which stacks two requests instead of one focused follow-up."
- **D3-A.** "The reply should stay on that behavioral question and ask how it was resolved." The judge also scored listening 1: "it does not focus specifically on the outcome the applicant left unanswered."

This is the reverse of run `455c41`, where judge-v1 passed Mini's "What did you do to help resolve it once you brought the charge nurse in?". The counting rule scores that reply "no", and Nishanth rejected it. Both cases fall under known limit 2 in `docs/judge-v1-known-limits.md`. This report does not reconcile them.

## D4-B: agreed rejection, false critical flags

- **Why both reject it.** The applicant had already said the brother "finally knew what to expect", so "What was the result after that?" repeats a question the applicant answered. Nishanth's reason is in `label_notes.md`.
- **The judge's verdict.** Next action 0, total 4/8, not acceptable.
- **The critical entries.** The judge also listed all four critical types, each with a rationale that denies the violation, for example "No clinical or legal advice is given."
  - Nishanth reviewed each one and found all four absent (`runs/20261121T195552Z-6237ea/critical_review.md`).
  - Under `scorer-1` the four entries count, so the raw decision names four critical violations that did not occur.
  - The verdict is kept as recorded.
