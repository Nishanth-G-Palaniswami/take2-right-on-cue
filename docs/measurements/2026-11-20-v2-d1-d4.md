# Measurement: v2 on D3/D4 (five replies each) and D1/D2 (two replies each)

Written and committed, together with `prompts/v2.md`, before any v2 reply was generated.

## The change

`prompts/v2.md` is `prompts/v1.md` with exactly one line replaced (line 25, the behavioral follow-up instruction). The wording is Nishanth's.

- **v1:** "If an answer is vague or incomplete, ask a short follow-up. When it's complete, move on."
- **v2:** "If part of your question is still unanswered, use what the applicant just said to ask one short follow-up about the missing part. Once the applicant has answered the question, briefly acknowledge their answer and move on."

The conversational-phrasing edit ("Tell me about a time…" instead of reading "Describe a time…") is deliberately left out. It belongs to a separate experiment, so the effect of this change can be attributed to it alone.

## Setup

Same as the v1 five-sample run (`docs/measurements/2026-11-20-v1-d3-d4-x5.md`): `gpt-5.4-mini`, reasoning effort `none`, independent samples, the same case files.

- Run 1: v2 × D3, D4 × 5 samples.
- Run 2: v2 × D1, D2 × 2 samples.

Nishanth labels the replies blind and his sheets are committed before Claude reports any count.

## Counting rules (fixed before generation)

**D3: did the reply ask for the missing outcome?** (count out of 5; compared with v1's 1 of 5)

The rule is unchanged from the v1 run:

- **Yes** if it asks how the situation ended, how it was resolved or turned out, or how the brother responded afterwards. It counts whether this is the whole question or part of one conversational request for the rest of the story.
- **No** if it asks only what the applicant or the team did, without asking about the result, or if it moves on.
- Replies asking "what did you do to resolve it" ask about actions, not the result, so they count as no. Each such borderline reply is quoted.

**D3 flag: did the reply move on early?** Flag any D3 reply that moves on (to the advocacy question, applicant questions, the close or any other next step) without asking a follow-up about the family story. This is the main regression risk of the v2 wording.

**D4: did the reply ask again about the resolved incident?** (count out of 5; compared with v1's 2 of 5)

The rule is unchanged from the v1 run:

- **Yes** if it asks any further question about the family story (its outcome, actions, feelings or lessons) instead of moving on.
- **No** if it acknowledges the answer and moves on to the advocacy question, or to another next step, without a question about the incident.

**D1 regression check** (out of 2): does the reply say that the call is recorded and shared with the recruiting team, and ask for permission to continue, without starting screening?

**D2 regression check** (out of 2): does the reply ask whether BLS and ACLS are current?

Each reply's classification is quoted next to Nishanth's label. Five samples per case give a rough frequency, not a rate estimate; a difference of one or two replies out of five can be chance.

### Reading the comparison

- D4 is the clearest change: 0 of 5 reopened the incident, against 2 of 5 for v1 (3 of 9 across all v1 runs). If v2 reopened as often as v1 did overall, seeing 0 of 5 would still happen about 13% of the time by chance ((6/9)^5 ≈ 0.13), so this is encouraging, not conclusive.
- D3 moved by one reply (2 of 5 against 1 of 5), which is within chance at this sample size. The main risk of the v2 wording, moving on early, did not occur.
- Not counted, for the separate phrasing experiment: 4 of 5 v2 D4 replies read "Describe a time…" verbatim.
